# Fictional example: a workshop announcement before files exist

An organizer is planning a free public workshop on reading charts. They have
already specified audience (adult beginners), venue/date/time, registration URL,
accessibility contact, a two-sentence abstract, and permission to publish those
details on the community noticeboard. Attendee identities and internal planning
remain private. They ask for a content map, not a poster or website yet.

A useful response reuses these answers and proposes:

|Material function|Priority|Destination and permission|Next action / urgency|
|---|---|---|---|
|Title, beginner audience, abstract|Essential|Public noticeboard, authorized|Plan concise invitation copy / before share|
|Date/time/venue and registration link|Essential|Public noticeboard, authorized|Plan a readable logistics block / before share|
|Accessibility contact|Useful|Public noticeboard, authorized|Plan a short contact line / before share|
|Optional decorative image|Accessory|Rights not established|Use an original text-only design or clarify rights if the image is actually wanted / ordinary|
|Attendee roster and internal planning|Out of scope|Private only|Keep outside proposed public content / ordinary unless actual exposure is found|

There is no need to ask the organizer for the already supplied date or publish
permission. Do not create a roster, website, slide deck or mandatory folder tree
because a template lists them. Do not invent hashes for nonexistent files. The
future rendered poster/text still needs inspection before sharing. If the venue
were genuinely unknown, one grouped logistics question could be necessary while
other content planning proceeds.
